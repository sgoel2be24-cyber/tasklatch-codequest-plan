## Claim discipline

Use "proposed," "planned," "target," or "to test" wherever a reader could otherwise infer that the prototype exists. Do not use fictional customer counts, percentages of harm reduced, invoices avoided, production safety guarantees, or an unverified $3,000 spending anecdote.

The slide-level sources belong in speaker notes. Material caveats belong visibly on the slide as well: self-reported survey, a finite target rather than a test result, local containers rather than microVMs, and simulated prices rather than a paid bill.

## Internal judging lens, without fabricated official weights

| Judging-relevant question | What the deck needs to make assessable |
| --- | --- |
| Is the problem clear? | A specific user and an operation that crosses from research into a consequential write |
| Is the solution differentiated? | Exact enforcement plus outcome evidence in a bounded local workflow, compared honestly with existing controls |
| Can the team build it? | Two workers, one fixture application, no paid infrastructure prerequisite, early capability checks |
| Can judges verify it? | Source-backed claims and a test plan measuring both useful completion and blocked forbidden actions |
| Who benefits? | Small teams and learners delegating a controlled browser workflow, with impact framed as hypotheses |
| Can it grow? | Runtime and action adapters as future seams, with explicit limits of the first version |
